Title: Study on the extrapolability of artificial neural network for predicting convective heat transfer of supercritical fluid based on a small number of samples
Authors: Qingjun Wang; Yu Chen; Feilong Dou; Yaheng Song; Yufeng Wang
Year 2026; International Journal of Thermal Sciences 224, 110698; DOI 10.1016/j.ijthermalsci.2026.110698
Domain: supercritical n-decane in electrically heated thin tube (2/3 mm, 800 mm) - NOT DPHE.
Data: experiments + 1D model for bulk properties; training dataset 410 sets (m 0.94-2.5 g/s, P 3-4 MPa, Qw 30-609 kW/m2, Tin 293-315 K); SEPARATE extrapolation test dataset 940 sets from new experiments (m to 4.8 g/s, P 5 MPa, Qw to 1528, Tin to 475 K) - genuine out-of-domain testing.
Models: Net1 dimensionless (inputs dimensionless groups incl. property-ratio corrections -> Nu; Tw recovered via surface-intersection solver) vs Net2 parametric (m, P, Qw, Tin, x... -> Tw directly). Depth 3-5 hidden layers x width 10-500 explored; tanh; lr 0.01 halved; 80/20 within training dataset for in-domain test.
Results: in-domain similar errors; on extrapolation dataset Net1 more accurate and physically consistent; Net2 produced non-physical Tw < Tb; with fewer training samples (e.g., 200) Net1 robust, Net2 degrades. Net1 better than 3 literature correlations on extrapolated data.
UQ: experimental uncertainties (m 3%, Qw 4.37%); no predictive intervals.
Relevance: strongest evidence in set that dimensionless/physics-structured inputs improve extrapolation with small data; supports Nu-based learned target + physics to derive other outputs.
Tier 3 (supporting methodology; high value for feature design and extrapolation policy).
