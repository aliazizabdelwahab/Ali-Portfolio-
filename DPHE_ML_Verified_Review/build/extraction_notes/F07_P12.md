Title: Prediction of heat transfer coefficient, pressure drop, and overall cost of double-pipe heat exchangers using the artificial neural network
Authors: Andaç Batur Çolak; Özgen Açıkgöz; Hatice Mercan; Ahmet Selim Dalkılıç; Somchai Wongwises
Year 2022; Case Studies in Thermal Engineering 39, 102391; DOI 10.1016/j.csite.2022.102391 (CC BY-NC-ND)
HX: multi double-pipe (hairpin) HX design calculation; fluids (several pure fluids and nanofluids) in tube side, water in annulus.
Data source: ANALYTICAL/parametric - 438 datasets generated from design correlations (h1 = 3373(1+0.0014tm)w^0.85, Hausen/Gnielinski annulus Nu, U from resistances incl. fouling 1e-4, cost model) from authors' previous papers. Not experimental.
Inputs Model 1 (10): rho, np (parallel tubes), k1, Re1, fi, Re2, fo, ns, P1, P2 (pumping powers); Model 2 (8): without P1, P2. Outputs: Uo (thermal), dP1, dP2 (hydraulic), Csum (cost).
Model: MLP, 1 hidden layer 15 neurons, tansig/purelin, LM; MATLAB. Neurons chosen by comparing performance (test likely involved).
Split: 70/15/15 random (306/66/66).
Metrics: R > 0.98; MSE Uo best 1.11e-4 (model 2; scale/normalisation not stated); mean deviations Uo 0.16% (M1), 0.02% (M2). All 438 points "on zero error line".
Critical: target is a deterministic function of inputs (many inputs are intermediate quantities of the same correlations, e.g., Re, f) -> near-perfect accuracy is expected and not evidence of predictive skill on physical data; "noise" discussion conflates redundancy with noise. No experimental data, no uncertainty.
Relevance: low-moderate (DPHE, nanofluid-property inputs, U as thermal target); shows that learning correlation-generated data inflates accuracy.
Tier 3 (analytical data; supporting/cautionary).
